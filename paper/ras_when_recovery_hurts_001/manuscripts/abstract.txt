Recovery interventions on frozen robot policies are usually judged by the failures they repair. The same intervention can also turn an episode that the policy would have completed into a failure, and an average success gain hides that second effect. We study this for a diffusion policy that stacks cubes in simulation. At the moment the policy is about to release the held cube, an observation-only controller (Vision-CENTER V2) estimates the horizontal offset between the two cubes from a base and a wrist RGB camera and a short visual memory, and either abstains or makes a bounded horizontal move before handing control back. Each scene is branched from one simulator snapshot, so intervened and uninterrupted runs are paired, and we count rescues (failure to success) and harms (success to failure) separately. After development, two pre-registered confirmatory stages used new scenes. On 400 calibration scenes per policy, harm was 1 of 348 and 0 of 337 baseline successes, and an exact binomial test with Holm correction rejected a conditional harm rate above 5% for both policies (δ = 0.05). On 400 held-out test scenes per policy, run once, success rose from 350 to 379 and from 347 to 373 (29 rescues vs 0 harms, p = 1.9×10⁻⁹; 27 vs 1, p = 1.1×10⁻⁷). We also report a failed physics-validity check, a memory ablation and a second policy architecture. The certificate is statistical and task-level; it is not a physical safety guarantee.
