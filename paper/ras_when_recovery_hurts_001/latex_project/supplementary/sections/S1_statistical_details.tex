\section{Statistical details}\label{sec:s-stats}

\paragraph{Sampling model and its assumption} The unit of analysis is the scene. Scene seeds of a split are distinct integers; each seed determines the cube placements through the simulator's reset, and the policy's sampling noise is keyed to the scene and the control step, identically for all branches up to the trigger. We \emph{assume} that the scenes of a split are independent draws from the scene distribution $\mathcal{D}$ of the task. Distinct seeds do not prove independence; the assumption rests on the simulator's seed-to-placement map, and a placement check confirmed only that no calibration or test scene repeats a placement of any earlier scene (0 of 2,347 references).

\paragraph{Conditional binomial argument for the certificate} Let $(Y_0^{(i)},Y_g^{(i)})$, $i=1,\dots,n$, be i.i.d. Write $S=\{i: Y_0^{(i)}=1\}$ and $n_+=|S|$. Given $S$, the variables $(1-Y_g^{(i)})_{i\in S}$ are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli with parameter $h(g)=\Pr(Y_g=0\mid Y_0=1)$, because the pairs are independent across scenes and each pair's conditional law depends only on its own $Y_0$. Hence $k=\sum_{i\in S}(1-Y_g^{(i)})\sim\Bin(n_+,h)$ conditionally on $S$. The p-value $p_{\mathrm{harm}}=F_{\Bin(n_+,\varepsilon)}(k)$ satisfies, for every $h\ge\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl(p_{\mathrm{harm}}\le a \bigm| S\bigr)\le a\qquad\text{for all } a\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
because the binomial distribution function at a fixed $k$ decreases in $h$; it is therefore valid for $H_0: h\ge\varepsilon$, including the boundary $h=\varepsilon$. Averaging over $S$ gives the unconditional statement. Holm's step-down procedure over the two policies controls the family-wise error at $\delta$ without assumptions on the dependence between the two p-values. The resulting guarantee is: if the conditional harm of the deployed system is at least $\varepsilon$ for some policy, the probability of certifying that policy is at most $\delta$. It is a property of the procedure over repeated calibration samples, not a probability statement about the true rate.

\paragraph{Exact paired efficacy test} With $b$ rescues and $c$ harms, $d=b+c$, the conditional law of $b$ given $d$ is $\Bin(d,\theta)$ with $\theta=\Pr(\text{rescue})/(\Pr(\text{rescue})+\Pr(\text{harm}))$. The null hypothesis of no improvement is $\theta\le1/2$, and $p_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Pr(\Bin(d,1/2)\ge b)$, one-sided. For $b=29,c=0$, $p=2^{-29}=1.86\times10^{-9}$; for $b=27,c=1$, $p=(28+1)\cdot2^{-28}=1.08\times10^{-7}$.

\paragraph{Intersection--union test for $H_{\mathrm{mem}}$} The memory hypothesis requires an improvement for both policies. The intersection--union test rejects only when both policy-level nulls are rejected at the same level; its p-value is the larger of the two, $\max(0.0059,0.0078)=0.0078$.

\paragraph{Power considerations} With $\varepsilon=0.05$, the certificate passes at the Holm level $0.025$ for up to 8 harms ($n_+=337$) or 9 harms ($n_+=348$), and at level $0.05$ for up to 10 harms (Eq.~(3) of the main text); the observed 1 and 0 harms are far from that boundary.
