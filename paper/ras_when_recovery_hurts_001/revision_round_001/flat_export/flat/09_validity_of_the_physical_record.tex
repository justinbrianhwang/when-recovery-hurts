\section{Validity of the physical record}\label{sec:physics}

\paragraph{Question} The mechanism analysis uses logged poses, contacts and distances. Do these measurements agree with each other, and does any disagreement change a result?

\paragraph{Quantities} Four quantities are involved, and they are not interchangeable. (i) \emph{Logged poses} of every robot link and cube at the end of each control step. (ii) The \emph{reconstructed signed distance} between two collision shapes, computed by us from those poses and the logged collision geometry (negative means overlap). (iii) The \emph{PhysX contact list}: PhysX lists every shape pair within the sum of their contact offsets (here 40\,mm), with a separation per contact point (signed, in metres) and an impulse per point; a listed pair with zero impulse is proximity, not contact \cite{nvidia_physx_contact}. (iv) \emph{Object displacement}, from the logged cube poses. Contact forces are the impulses divided by the time step.

\paragraph{Checks} Three checks on these quantities were defined before the analysis (Table~\ref{tab:physics}). \emph{S4}: at every logged pose during a move, the reconstructed distance between any robot shape and the target cube is positive. \emph{S5}: every shape pair with a non-zero contact impulse is reconstructed no more than 1\,mm apart; the rule is one-sided, and overlaps (penetrations) are reported without a threshold, because PhysX lets bodies interpenetrate under load. \emph{S6}: for every listed shape pair whose PhysX separation is positive, in all phases of a branch, the PhysX separation and the reconstructed distance differ by at most 1\,mm. S4 and S5 concern logged poses only; none of the checks establishes continuous-time collision freedom.

\paragraph{Evidence: S4 and S5} S4 failed in one of 715 acted branches and in none of the V2 branches. The failing branch is the oracle in scene 51152 (policy seed 2), where the trigger fired while the fingers held the \emph{target} cube: the reconstructed finger--target distance was $-2.16$\,mm already at the trigger, with about 28\,N of contact force on each finger, so the S4 violation records the wrong grasp rather than a collision caused by the move. The oracle then pushed the target 24\,mm and enlarged the offset; V2 abstained in this scene. S5 failed in 11 of 3,874 branch records (400 trunks plus 9 arms in each of 386 triggered scene-runs), which share 2 distinct events with fast-moving cubes (scene 51143, seed 2, and scene 51270, seed 1). Replacing every logged link mesh by its convex hull changed no S4 or S5 status (Fig.~\ref{fig:hull}); it changed hand--cube distances by at most 0.24\,mm and finger distances not at all.

\paragraph{Evidence: S6} S6 \emph{failed}: on the original development record, 54,267 of 580,539 rows of separated listed pairs (9.3\%) disagreed by more than 1\,mm (Fig.~\ref{fig:s6}a). An independent second reconstruction on a subset agreed with ours to numerical precision for box shapes and to within 0.24\,mm for hand--cube pairs, so the disagreement lies between PhysX and the logged poses, not between our two reconstructions.

\paragraph{Partial explanation (post hoc)} Three follow-up measurements, each on different data, explain part of the S6 failure. First, in a static experiment with two boxes of known geometry, the PhysX separation equals the analytic gap before the last sub-step within 0.05\,mm in all 56 of 59 cases that produced listed points (Fig.~\ref{fig:s6}c). Second, a re-run of 11 used development scene-runs on the calibration host, with every sub-step logged, showed that the contact list is computed at the pose before the last of the five sub-steps of a control step; comparing with that pose reduced the separated pairs differing by more than 1\,mm from 5,181 to 1,874 of 56,558 (from 9.2\% to 3.3\%), and for pairs faster than 100\,mm/s from 3,436 to 146 (Fig.~\ref{fig:s6}b). These re-run traces differ from the original development traces (host difference), so this result describes the simulator, not the original record. Third, the remaining 1,874 rows are all zero-impulse proximity pairs between a finger box and a cube; shape-index mismatch and static edge geometry were excluded as causes, and their origin is not established.

\paragraph{Interpretation} The task outcomes, the harm certificate and the efficacy test use only the success flag of each branch, which the simulator computes from its own state; they do not depend on S4--S6. The S6 failure limits the use of logged separations for fine-grained contact analysis, which is why they are used only descriptively here.

\paragraph{Limits} S6 keeps its official FAIL status, and a residual of 3.3\% in the sub-step re-run is unexplained. Distances are evaluated at logged poses only; a contact between control steps can be missed.
